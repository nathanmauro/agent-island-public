The fixture harbor opened early and the gulls were already loud.
A delivery van idled by the gate while its driver checked a list.
Nobody on the pier seemed in a hurry to start the day.
Two cyclists argued mildly about the fastest way into town.
The bakery on the corner had put out its chalkboard sign.
It promised fresh bread by nine and soup by noon.
A dog slept in the one patch of sun near the fountain.
Somewhere a radio played an old song with the volume too low.
The tide was out, leaving ropes slack against the stones.
A fisherman mended a net without looking at his hands.
Children walked past in pairs on their way to school.
One of them dropped a glove and ran back to get it.
The clock tower showed a time that nobody trusted.
Clouds moved in slowly from the west over the hills.
A shop owner swept the same square of pavement twice.
The ferry horn sounded once from somewhere out of sight.
Pigeons gathered where someone had scattered crumbs.
A window above the pharmacy was propped open with a book.
The morning went on quietly in every direction.
Nothing in particular happened, and that was fine.
